Road maintenance works best when small defects are recorded before they become expensive repairs.

Potholes commonly develop when water enters cracks, weakens the road base, and is pushed upward by repeated vehicle traffic. Drainage checks, crack sealing, and timely patching can reduce the chance of a small defect growing into a larger surface failure.

Damaged road signs should be repaired because clear signs help drivers understand speed limits, turns, crossings, and temporary hazards. A missing or unreadable sign can make a predictable road condition harder to navigate safely.

A practical maintenance cycle includes inspecting roads after severe weather, recording the location and type of each issue, prioritizing hazards that affect visibility or vehicle control, and checking completed repairs during a later inspection.
